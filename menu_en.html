1893 · Where it all began

In 1893, Doña Micaela Aquino began selling chocolate and enchiladas in front of the San Juan de Dios church, next to the market that every Saturday brought together the people of what was then known as Verde Antequera.

By her side was her daughter, Apolonia López Aquino, who over time continued the family tradition and carried forward the flavors that would shape our cuisine: chocolate, enchiladas and Oaxaca’s traditional moles.

How “La Abuelita” got its name

When Doña Apolonia was already running her stand in Mercado Benito Juárez, journalist and historian Arcelia Yañiz, a regular Sunday customer, asked her what her business was called.

“I still haven’t given it a name,” replied Pola, as she was affectionately known. Doña Arcelia then suggested: “Why don’t you call it La Abuelita? You already have gray hair and grandchildren.”

And so, from a simple and affectionate conversation, the name our family still proudly carries was born.

From the market to a tradition

Later, when Mercado 20 de Noviembre was built, the family received the spaces that became home to the comedor. Since then, generation after generation has preserved the recipes, cooking methods and warm hospitality that define La Abuelita.

Today, we continue preparing our food to order, honoring the flavors of traditional Oaxacan cuisine and sharing a part of our story with every guest.

Five generations later…

After more than 100 years, Comedor Típico La Abuelita continues to preserve the flavors of traditional Oaxacan cuisine. Among our most representative dishes is the “La Abuelita” Special Soup, a family recipe that has been part of our story for generations.

Thank you for being part of our story.

It is a pleasure to welcome you to our table and share the flavors our family has cared for through five generations.
